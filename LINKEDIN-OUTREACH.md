# LinkedIn Outreach — Scripts & Sequence

> Companion to `public/documents/LinkedIn-Outreach-Playbook.pdf`.
> Replace every `[bracket]` before sending. If you cannot fill a bracket with something specific,
> do not send the message.

---

## Limits to respect

| Channel | Limit | Practical target |
|---|---|---|
| Connection request note | 300 characters | 180–260 |
| InMail subject | ~200 characters | under 60 |
| InMail body | ~1,900 characters | 600–900 |
| Message to a connection | 8,000 characters | 400–700 |

Send **20–25 invitations a week**. Near the weekly cap looks automated.
No bots or automation tools — that is the fastest route to a restricted account.

## Connection notes (no pitch, just the accept)

**A — founder, product-specific**
```
Hi [Name] — I build multi-tenant SaaS platforms (7 shipped, 3 live). Saw [Company] is scaling [specific thing]; happy to share how we handled [specific problem] without adding headcount. Worth connecting?
```

**B — hiring manager**
```
Hi [Name] — saw you're hiring a [role]. I've shipped 7 production platforms on Next.js and TypeScript, end to end. Happy to send the three questions I'd ask every candidate for that role. Worth connecting?
```

**C — via a shared connection**
```
Hi [Name] — [Mutual] suggested I reach out. I build SaaS platforms end to end (7 shipped, 3 live). Not pitching anything — I'd just like to stay connected in case [topic] comes up on your side.
```

**D — after their post**
```
Hi [Name] — your post on [topic] was the clearest take I've read on it. I've been dealing with the same problem at [Company]. Would like to follow what you ship next.
```

**E — no context, keep it light**
```
Hi [Name] — I work on multi-tenant SaaS and AI workflows out of Lahore. Your work on [thing] caught my eye. Happy to connect and swap notes on [topic].
```

## InMail (for people you cannot connect with)

**Client**
```
Subject: Your [product] + [specific feature]

Hi [Name],

You added [specific feature] to [Product] last month — that's the part I'd have built first too.

I build multi-tenant SaaS platforms end to end: 7 shipped, 3 live, 500+ tenants. The closest thing to your setup was [project], where [one concrete result].

I'm not asking for a project. I'd like 15 minutes to show you two things I'd change in [specific area] — you keep the ideas either way.

Does Thursday or Friday work better?

— Kashif
```

**Hiring manager**
```
Subject: [Role] — shipped 7 platforms, 3 live

Hi [Name],

Saw the [role] post. Rather than send a CV cold, one thing that might be useful: I've shipped 7 production platforms (Next.js, TypeScript, PostgreSQL), three of them live with real tenants today.

For a role like yours the relevant part is probably [specific requirement from their post] — I did exactly that at [project], where [result].

If it's still open, I'd welcome 15 minutes. If it's filled, I'd still like to know what the team is building.

— Kashif
```

**Partnership**
```
Subject: Idea for [Company] + [Partner]

Hi [Name],

You and [Partner] serve the same customer from different angles — [observation].

I build the systems in the middle: multi-tenant SaaS, integrations, AI workflows. I've done this for [comparable situation].

Worth a short call to see if there's something real here? If not, I'll leave you alone.

— Kashif
```

## Follow-up sequence

| When | What | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Connection note or InMail | Accept or read |
| Day 1–2 | Thanks + context, no ask | Become a person |
| Day 4 | One specific observation about their product | Give value first |
| Day 9 | The ask — 15 minutes | Book the call |
| Day 16 | Clean close-out | Leave the door open |

**Day 1–2**
```
Thanks for connecting, [Name]. Quick context so I'm not a stranger in your feed: I build multi-tenant SaaS platforms — 7 shipped, 3 live in production. Currently working on [thing] at [Company].

No ask. Just thought [specific thing about them] was worth following.
```

**Day 4**
```
[Name] — I was on [Product] this morning and noticed [specific observation]. If you're already aware, ignore me. If not, it's probably costing [specific cost] on [specific flow].

Happy to send the one-page write-up I made for a similar case — no charge.
```

**Day 9**
```
[Name] — I'll be direct since you're busy: I have room for one build next month, and [Company] is the kind of problem I'd want to work on.

Fifteen minutes to see if there's a fit? If the timing is wrong, tell me and I'll check back in [quarter].
```

**Day 16**
```
[Name] — I'll stop here so I'm not cluttering your inbox. If [specific thing] becomes a priority later, I'm easy to find.

Good luck with [specific thing they're doing].
```

Three follow-ups, then stop. Silence is usually timing, not rejection.

## Job track

**Recruiter**
```
Subject: [Role] — 7 platforms shipped, 3 live

Hi [Name],

I applied for [role] on [date] and wanted to put a face to the application.

Short version: 7 production SaaS platforms shipped, 3 live with real tenants, full stack from PostgreSQL schema to deployment. The closest match to your spec is [project] — [one result].

Happy to answer anything the CV doesn't cover.

— Kashif
```

**Hiring manager**
```
Subject: One question about [role]

Hi [Name],

You're hiring a [role]. I'll skip the pitch and ask the question I'd want answered if I were you: is the priority shipping [specific thing] in the next quarter, or stabilising what already exists?

I've done both — most recently [example with a number]. If the first one is the answer, we should talk.

— Kashif
```

**Referral request**
```
Subject: Quick favour — [Company] [role]

Hi [Name],

You're at [Company] and they're hiring a [role]. I'm not going to ask you to vouch for me blind.

If you're open to it, I'll send you a three-line summary and my CV — you can forward it or ignore it with no hard feelings.

Either way, good to be connected.
```

## Objection handling

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "Send your CV" | "Sent — plus a one-page case study of the closest thing I've built for you: [link]. If it's useful, I can walk through the architecture in 15 minutes." |
| "What's your rate?" | "Most builds like this land between [X] and [Y] depending on scope. I can send a fixed-scope quote after a 15-minute call — that way you're not comparing guesses." |
| "We're not hiring now" | "Understood. I'll check back in [quarter]. In the meantime, here's a short teardown of one thing I noticed in [product] — no charge, no strings." |
| "We already have a team" | "Makes sense. Teams usually get stuck on [specific thing] rather than general capacity. If that becomes the bottleneck, I do short engagements for exactly that." |
| "Too expensive" | "Fair. What range are you working with? I'll tell you honestly what's realistic at that number instead of quietly shrinking the scope." |
| "Let me think about it" | "Of course. I'll follow up on [specific date]. If it's easier, here's the one-pager so you have the details when you need them." |
| No reply at all | Send the day-16 close-out. Do not send a fourth follow-up. |

**Four rules:** reply within 24 hours · never argue · always end with a next step or a clean exit ·
change scope, not price.

## Tracking

Use `linkedin-assets/outreach-tracker.csv` (opens in Excel or Google Sheets):

`name, company, role, URL, signal, channel, first message date, status, next action date, notes, outcome`

Review every Friday. No reply after three touches → mark *dormant*, revisit next quarter.

After every call, ask: *"Would you be open to a two-line note about what we discussed?"*
That is how the recommendation count on your profile grows without asking for a favour.
